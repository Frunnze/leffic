Action potentials — note

Resting state

A neuron at rest sits near −70 mV. The sodium–potassium pump moves three sodium ions out for every two potassium ions in, and potassium leak channels let potassium drift back out.

Depolarisation

When a stimulus pushes the membrane past roughly −55 mV, voltage-gated sodium channels open and sodium floods in. The membrane potential swings positive, peaking near +40 mV.

Repolarisation

  • Sodium channels inactivate.
  • Voltage-gated potassium channels open.
  • Potassium leaves, driving the potential back down.

Refractory period

Potassium channels close slowly, so the membrane briefly overshoots below rest. During this window the neuron cannot fire again, which is what keeps signals travelling in one direction.
